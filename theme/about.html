__STANDFIRST__

Each piece here takes a passage from a teacher and works it against a stretch of an ordinary day. The passage is the fixed part. The writing around it gets re-rendered as the practice changes shape, so a piece carries both the date it was first sent and the revision shape it currently stands in.

The practice rests on three people who never met and shared no tradition. David Foster Wallace named the thing, in a commencement address called “This Is Water”: a default setting that construes everything through the self. Shunryu Suzuki had already measured it — in our everyday life, our thinking is ninety-nine percent self-centered. Śaṅkara, eight centuries earlier, supplied the mechanism: superimposition, the rope taken for a snake in dim light, removed by seeing rather than by courage.

Three roads, no contact, one diagnosis. That convergence is the standard any new source gets measured against, and it is why the writing keeps returning to the same four pillars.

The framework

Four pillars, and under them the movement all four sit inside. Every article declares one of these, and the count beside it is how many pieces stand there so far. A pillar nobody has written under yet is still part of the framework and still listed — the gap is the point, not an omission.

Reading the stamps

Under each title on the index sits a line like Sent 2026–09–15 · Shape 21. Sent is the day the piece first went out, and it never changes. Shape is the revision shape the writing currently stands in — the passage at the center of a piece stays fixed while the writing around it is re-rendered, so the two dates say different things on purpose.

There is no schedule and no argument being built. A passage, a stretch of an ordinary day, and what happens when the two are held against each other.
