title: Let us leave it at no
pillar: The movement — effort to look, no effort in the seeing

Ask yourself a question about yourself and something answers. A description comes up — this is the kind of person I am, this is why I did that. The Brihadâranyaka has an instruction for what to do with the description when it arrives, and the instruction is to deny it. Let's read the sentence.

> "Next follows the teaching (of Brahman) by No, no! for there is nothing else higher than this (if one says): 'It is not so.' Then comes the name 'the True of the True,' the senses being the True, and he (the Brahman) the True of them."
>
> — Brihadâranyaka Upanishad II.3.6, translated by Max Müller

The teaching is a denial and it stops there. What comes before this sentence in the text is a list of descriptions — saffron cloth, white wool, a flame, a white lotus, a flash of lightning — one comparison after another for what the innermost thing looks like. Then the sentence above arrives and takes them all off the table. Not because those pictures were poor and better ones exist. The text says there is nothing else higher than saying it is not so, which means the denial is not a step on the way to the right description. It is where the looking ends.

Taking the description that arrives is the first option, and it works. It fits the facts, it explains the morning, and you can use it in the next conversation. What it costs is that you now have a self with properties, and a self with properties has to be run. Every situation picks up a second job — doing the thing and managing the character who does it. And a description can be argued with, so from now on it gets defended.

Replacing it with a better description is the second option. No, not that, really I'm the one who — and a truer sentence goes in. It costs the same as the first, paid later. You answered the same question in the same currency, and something did move, so it feels like you got somewhere. What moved was the sentence. The one producing sentences never came into view, because it was busy producing.

Saying no and putting nothing in its place is the third option, and it costs you the answer. You asked who is doing this and nothing came back. There is no line to carry out of the moment, nothing to tell anyone, nothing to tell yourself. From the inside that is very close to never having asked.

So what are we aiming at. We want to say no to the answer and not reach for the next one.

*Let us see where this shows up in an ordinary day. Three places.*

- Someone at home says you always do a certain thing, and a sentence about yourself comes up to meet it.
- You are halfway through being irritated, you ask why you are like this, and an answer arrives.
- You are lying awake going over something you said, and a line about the kind of person who says that arrives with it.

In all three the thing to watch is the same. Watch the gap right after the no — whether something comes forward to take the place of what you just denied, or whether nothing comes.

You are not deciding anything in that second, and you are not trying to keep the gap empty. Watch what arrives in it, and leave it as it is.
