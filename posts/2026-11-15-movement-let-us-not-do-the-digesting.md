title: Let us not do the digesting
pillar: The movement — effort to look, no effort in the seeing

Something you read turns out to be true, and a moment later you are looking for what to do with it. Epictetus wrote about that moment, and the picture he reached for is a sheep in a field. Let's take the sheep slowly, because there are three things in it and one of them is the one we keep trying to perform ourselves.

> For even sheep do not vomit up their grass and show to the shepherds how much they have eaten; but when they have internally digested the pasture, they produce externally wool and milk. Do you also show not your theorems to the uninstructed, but show the acts which come from their digestion.
>
> — Epictetus, Enchiridion 46

The sheep does two things and does not do the third. It eats, and later there is wool. In between sits the digesting, and no sheep has ever digested on purpose. Epictetus marks that stretch with two words, internally and externally, and they are doing real work: the conversion happens somewhere we cannot get at, and what comes out is not what went in. Wool is not grass in a tidier arrangement. It is another substance altogether, made by a process nobody attended.

Hold the three things we do with a line that has landed, and look at what each one costs.

We say it back. The line comes out in the shape it went in, which is the one shape that proves nothing happened to us on the way through. It is available because it is unchanged, and we mistake the availability for having taken it in. Grass is still grass.

We work on it. We turn it over, make a method of it, carry it around and check in on whether it has taken yet. This is the honest-looking one, and the cost is in the handling itself. Holding a line up to examine keeps it in front of us as an object for as long as we keep working, and the one holding it there is the self, so the whole stretch is us and the line and nothing else in the room. The check cannot come back clean either. Looking inward to see whether we have changed is an operation on ourselves, not on the day, and it is the same self doing the looking and the reporting.

We eat and go on with the afternoon. The cost of this one is that nothing shows. There is no reading to take, wool arrives in a season rather than in a morning, and from the inside a season of digesting looks exactly like a season of forgetting. The default setting reads no evidence as nothing happening, and will say so.

So what are we aiming at. We want what we take in to come back out as the way a thing gets done, rather than as the line about it. And the eating stays ours — the sheep is in the pasture all day, and nobody is relieved of the grazing. What is not ours is the part in the middle.

*Let us see where this shows up in an ordinary day. Three places.*

- You read something early that is plainly true, and by the middle of the day you catch yourself hunting for somewhere to use it.
- Someone tells you how they handle a thing at home, and you recognise it as right, and you are already wording it back to them.
- A few quiet minutes in the evening, and the mind returns to a line from earlier to see what it has done to you yet.

In all three the thing to watch is one question, and it has two answers. Am I eating, or am I bringing it back up to look at. The second one has a particular feel to it — the line is being held at arm's length and turned, and you are at the other end of the arm.

You are not deciding anything in that second, and you are not trying to make it come out the first way. Watch which one it is, and leave it as it is.
