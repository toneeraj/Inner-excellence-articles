A pupil asks his teacher who is in charge of the senses. He lists them as he goes — mind, breath, speech, eye, ear — and he wants to know which of them sends the others out on their work. The answer he gets does not name a sixth thing. It takes his own five words and puts each one behind itself.

> 1. THE Pupil asks: 'At whose wish does the mind sent forth proceed on its errand? At whose command does the first breath go forth? At whose wish do we utter this speech? What god directs the eye, or the ear?'
>
> 2. The Teacher replies: 'It is the ear of the ear, the mind of the mind, the speech of speech, the breath of breath, and the eye of the eye. When freed (from the senses) the wise, on departing from this world, become immortal.'
>
> — Talavakâra (Kena) Upanishad, First Khanda, verses 1 and 2, translated by Max Müller

Every candidate the pupil offers is an instrument that points outward, and he is asking which of the outward things runs the outward things. The teacher gives him back the same nouns, each one doubled. The eye of the eye. Whatever is doing the seeing is not a better eye and not the eye at full stretch. It sits one position behind the eye, and the eye is the thing it looks through.

Take that seriously and the ordinary repair stops making sense. When a read of something is off, the move we know is to work the instrument — look again, look longer, look harder at the same face or the same line. Effort put into an instrument is effort we can feel ourselves making, and the thing that grows under it is the sense of being someone who is looking carefully. The picture itself does not move. We looked twice and came away with the first read plus the memory of trying.

The second position is to stop and let the read stand, which costs more than it looks like costing. A read that has been closed becomes the facts. Nothing further gets in, and the relief is real, because a finished read asks nothing more of us and a person we have finished reading is easy to be around.

The third is the one the verse points at, and it has its own cost. One position behind the instrument there is nothing to do. No procedure, no stretch, nothing that registers as work — and from the inside, no effort at all is very hard to tell apart from not bothering. The verse offers no way to feel the difference while it is happening.

So what are we aiming at. We want the eye of the eye. Not a sharper eye, and not a closed one.

*Let us see where this shows up in an ordinary day. Three places.*

- A message reads cold, and you open it a second time to work out what they meant by it.
- You cannot find your keys, and you go back over the same counter, looking harder at a surface you have already looked at.
- Someone at the table repeats something you have heard them say before, and you lean in to hear it properly this time.

One thing to watch inside all three: in the second you decide to look again, notice where the effort goes — into what is in front of you, or into the looking.

You are not deciding anything in that second, and you are not trying to make the effort land in the right place. Watch what moves, and leave it as it is.
