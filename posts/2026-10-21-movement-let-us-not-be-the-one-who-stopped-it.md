title: Let us not be the one who stopped it
pillar: The movement — effort to look, no effort in the seeing
originally_sent: 2026-10-21
standfirst: Suzuki gives the stopping of a thought to the thought itself, which leaves the turning back to check as the one part that was ever ours.
sources:
  - Shunryu Suzuki, Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind, "Mind Waves" (1970).
---

Something comes into your mind, and a little later it has gone. Suzuki says who stopped it, in five words. Let's take the passage first and then that sentence on its own.

From Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind, the chapter called "Mind Waves":

> "When you are practicing zazen, do not try to stop your thinking. Let it stop by itself. If something comes into your mind, let it come in, and let it go out. It will not stay long. When you try to stop your thinking, it means you are bothered by it."
>
> — Shunryu Suzuki, Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind, "Mind Waves"

Let it stop by itself. The thing that does the stopping, in that sentence, is the thinking. Not you. You are not doing it slowly or doing it well. You are not in the sentence at all. The only verb left for you is let, and letting is the absence of a move.

The check afterwards has nothing to land on. A minute later you may want to go back into the second and find what you did — the letting-go, the not-being-bothered, the thing you could do again tomorrow. The one item in there that belongs to you is the going back. So that is what you find, and you come out holding a method. Suzuki has already said what a method is here: when you try to stop your thinking, it means you are bothered by it. A technique for letting go is a way of being bothered on a schedule.

The opposite move costs the other half. If nothing in that second was yours, it is a short step to leaving the second alone altogether — nothing asked, nothing turned over. The turn to look was the one part that was ever ours. Dropping it leaves the autopilot running with permission.

Letting the stopping belong to the thought costs the confirmation. Suzuki's word for how the waves settle is gradually — if you are not bothered by the waves, gradually they will become calmer and calmer. Gradually has no instant in it. There is no second at which it happened, so there is no second you can go to and ask.

So what are we aiming at. We want the stopping to be the thought's. Something comes in, we take no position against it, and it leaves on its own time with nothing of ours in the room.

*Let us see where this shows up in an ordinary day. Three places.*

- Someone says something at the table that lands badly, nothing comes of it, and in the next quiet minute you go back over it to see how you took it.
- A name comes up that you have no warm feeling about, the feeling passes, and you check whether it has really passed.
- You are lying in bed, the afternoon starts replaying, it fades, and you want to know whether you let it fade or just fell asleep.

One thing to watch in all three: the turning back. Not what you find there. The turn itself.

You are not deciding anything in that second, and you are not trying to make it come out the right way. Watch what moves, and leave it as it is.
