---
title: Let us not take it as news
pillar: P2 — This Is Water
originally_sent: 2026-11-02
standfirst: Suzuki says a thought only appears to come in from outside, and the aim is to tell something coming in from something coming up.
sources:
  - Shunryu Suzuki, "Mind Waves," Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind (1970).
---

Something comes into your mind and it comes in from somewhere. The morning did it, or the message did it, or the person at the table did it. Shunryu Suzuki wrote one line about that coming-in. Let's take it slowly.

> If something comes into your mind, let it come in, and let it go out. It will not stay long.
>
> It appears as if something comes from outside your mind, but actually it is only the waves of your mind, and if you are not bothered by the waves, gradually they will become calmer and calmer.
>
> — Shunryu Suzuki, Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind, "Mind Waves"

Suzuki uses the arrival language himself first. Something comes into your mind. Then he takes it back. It appears as if it came from outside, and it did not. The sense of having come in is part of the thought, arriving with it, made of the same water.

That direction is worth a great deal. A thing that came in from outside is information about outside. The irritation that turned up while you were reading the message is a report on the message. The heaviness that turned up in the kitchen is a report on the kitchen. Take the direction away and the thought is still there, still unpleasant, and no longer a witness to anything.

Take the direction as it comes, and every feeling is a finding. The day is then made of things that did something to you, and there is no way to tell a wave from a fact, because the two arrive by the same door wearing the same clothes. You will be right some of the time. You will have no way of knowing which times.

Discount the direction in advance, and you are running a filter. Everything that turns up gets held at the door and asked where it came from, which is a job, and it is a job done by you, about you, all day. It also throws out the true reports along with the rest. Sometimes the reply really was short.

Let it arrive, and notice that it arrived feeling like news. That costs the thought its standing. The irritation was going to explain the morning for you, and now it explains nothing, and nothing arrives to take its place. You are left holding a mood with no account attached, which is less comfortable than being right about the kitchen.

So what are we aiming at. We want to know the difference between something coming in and something coming up.

*Let us see where this shows up in an ordinary day. Three places.*

- You walk into the kitchen and within a few seconds you are annoyed, and the annoyance is about the kitchen.
- A short reply lands on your phone and you know what it means before the phone is back in your pocket.
- You wake up already heavy, and the heaviness has a reason attached, and the reason is yesterday.

In all three, something turned up with a direction on it. One thing to watch: whether it came in, or came up.

You are not deciding anything in that second, and you are not trying to send it back. Watch whether it felt like it came in, and leave it as it is.
