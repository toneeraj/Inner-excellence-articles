Iris Murdoch calls one thing by two different names, a few lines apart. The first is the word she has while she is inside it. The second turns up after it has gone, and nobody corrected her in between.

> "I am looking out of my window in an anxious and resentful state of mind, oblivious of my surroundings, brooding perhaps on some damage done to my prestige. Then suddenly I observe a hovering kestrel. In a moment everything is altered. The brooding self with its hurt vanity has disappeared. There is nothing now but kestrel."
>
> — Iris Murdoch, The Sovereignty of Good (1970)

Damage done to my prestige is the first word. Hurt vanity is the second. They are the same event. The first says something was done to her and it counts; the second says what was hurt was vanity. She learned no new fact in between — the kestrel told her nothing whatever about the matter. What changed is that the thing supplying the word was no longer there to supply it.

Take the word you have while you are in it. It is the only one on offer, and it is honest about the feeling: the heat is real and the injury is real to you. The cost is that every grievance arrives with its own certification, and the certifying is done by the party with the interest. Nothing accumulates, either. The thing deflates on its own schedule, you carry on with the smaller word, and the next one turns up at full size with its papers in order.

Discount it in advance instead — decide that whatever this is, it is probably vanity. You will be wrong a good deal of the time, because some damage is damage and wants attending to. And the discounting is run by the same self, so there is now a new thing to be right about: that you are not the sort to make a fuss. That can be brooded over just as well as the original.

Let the second word arrive on its own, and register that it arrived. The cost is the timing, which is not yours. It comes late. It does nothing for the thing you are inside at this minute, and it will not come any sooner next time for having come today. What you get is one instance of the two words sitting next to each other, in your own case, with your own material.

So what are we aiming at. We want the change of word to register. Not to pick the right word in the moment — in the moment there is only one word. We want to be people who notice, afterwards, that there were two.

*Let us see where this shows up in an ordinary day. Three places.*

- You come back to the kitchen after something interrupted you — the door, the phone, somebody needing something — and the thing you were turning over is still sitting there waiting for you.
- You wake up the next morning and remember what happened yesterday.
- Someone asks what is wrong and you start to say it out loud.

In all three the thing to watch is the same. Not whether it got smaller. Whether the word you reach for now is the word you had then.

You are not deciding anything in that second, and you are not trying to make the second word come. Watch which word you have, and leave it as it is.
