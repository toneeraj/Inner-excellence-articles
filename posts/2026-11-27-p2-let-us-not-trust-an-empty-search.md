title: Let us not trust an empty search
pillar: P2 — This Is Water
originally_sent: 2026-11-27
standfirst: The father in the Khandogya Upanishad asks his son to find salt dissolved in water and then to taste it, which shows that a search works by difference and comes back empty on whatever is spread through the whole.
sources:
  - Khandogya Upanishad, Sixth Prapâthaka, Thirteenth Khanda, translated by Max Müller
---

A father puts salt in water and leaves it overnight. In the morning he asks his son to bring the salt back, and the son looks for it and cannot find it. Then the father asks him to taste.

> 'Place this salt in water, and then wait on me in the morning.'
>
> The son did as he was commanded.
>
> The father said to him: 'Bring me the salt, which you placed in the water last night.'
>
> The son having looked for it, found it not, for, of course, it was melted.
>
> The father said: 'Taste it from the surface of the water. How is it?' The son replied: 'It is salt.' 'Taste it from the middle. How is it?' The son replied: 'It is salt.' 'Taste it from the bottom. How is it?' The son replied: 'It is salt.'
>
> Then the father said: 'Here also, in this body, forsooth, you do not perceive the True (Sat), my son; but there indeed it is.'
>
> — Khandogya Upanishad, Sixth Prapâthaka, Thirteenth Khanda, translated by Max Müller

Two operations are being asked for here and only the second one works. Looking is how we find a thing: we scan for the part that is not like the rest, and the part that stands out is the thing. The salt had gone into every part of the water, so no part of it was unlike any other part, and an honest search returned nothing. Tasting asks no part to stand out. Any mouthful answers.

The first position is to take the empty search as the answer. Nothing stood out, so there was nothing there, and this is the most believable verdict of the three, because the search was real and careful and we conducted it ourselves. An honest search is the hardest finding to reopen. There is nothing to reopen it with.

The second is to search again and search better. A search works by difference, which means a thing spread evenly through the whole has nothing left to be different from. The same nothing comes back, and it comes back with more authority each time, so the second sweep leaves us further from the salt than the first one did.

The third is the one the father asks for, and it costs the exhibit. A taste tells you the thing is there and hands you nothing to point at — no item, no part of the water you can lift out and show to someone who asks what you found. From the outside, and for a while from the inside, it looks the same as having found nothing.

*The father then has him throw the water away, and the text remarks that salt exists for ever.*

So what are we aiming at. We want to be able to say it is salt about a thing we have just failed to find.

*Let us see where this shows up in an ordinary day. Three places.*

- You read a message back to check it for anything that will land wrong, find nothing, and send it.
- Someone asks whether anything is bothering you, and you check, and the answer that comes back is no.
- You go through a plan for what has been left out, nothing is missing, and you call it done.

One thing to watch inside all three: in the second the search comes back empty, notice what you were searching for — something that would have stood out from the rest of it, or something that is in all of it.

You are not deciding anything in that second, and you are not trying to find something after all. Watch what moves, and leave it as it is.
