There is a suspicion sitting under all of this and it is worth saying out loud. If I stop minding how a thing turns out, won't I do it worse. Krsna comes to the end of his argument about letting the results go, and he answers that with one word. Let's take the verse the word is in.

> "Endued with this evenness of mind, one frees oneself in this life, alike from vice and virtue. Devote thyself, therefore, to this Yoga. Yoga is the very dexterity of work."
>
> — Bhagavad Gita 2.50, trans. Swami Swarupananda (Advaita Ashrama)

The word is dexterity. It is a word about hands, about doing a thing well. Krsna has spent three verses taking the fruits of the work away, and when he names what is left standing he names a competence. His claim is that the evenness and the skill are one thing, which puts the attention he removed somewhere other than on the work.

Keep the suspicion and keep minding how it comes out, on the grounds that the minding is what holds the standard up. Then the standard is tied to what is riding on the outcome. It stays high where something is riding on it and it sags where nothing is, and you have no way left to tell your skill apart from your stake.

Try to run both — mind the result, and give the thing in your hands everything else. Attention is one quantity, and the share of it watching how this will land is not on the thing. Whatever comes out, you cannot say which of the two made it.

Take Krsna at his word and the evenness is the skill. That costs the reassurance that caring hard about an outcome is the same as doing a thing well, and it costs the explanation we reach for when something comes out poorly. It also leaves his claim checkable in one kind of place only — where nothing is riding on the result, which is where we have the least appetite to look.

So what are we aiming at. We want our hands to be the same on the part that will not be looked at.

*Let us see where this shows up in an ordinary day. Three places.*

- The back of a shelf you are painting, the side that will face the wall.
- The washing-up at the end of the evening, when everyone else has gone to bed.
- A short message to somebody who will read it once and never mention it.

In all three the thing to watch is one second — the second your hands arrive at the part nobody will see. It has two answers. Did the care hold steady, or did it thin out.

You are not deciding anything in that second, and you are not trying to make the care hold. Watch whether it thins, and leave it as it is.
